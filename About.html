About Me

I am a PhD scholar at Beijing Forestry University, Beijing, China, with research interests in applied mathematics and fluid mechanics. My work focuses on the numerical analysis of linear and nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs), particularly in the study of hybrid nanofluids, entropy generation, and heat and mass transfer processes. Through my research, I aim to contribute to the development of efficient and sustainable energy systems using advanced mathematical modeling and computational techniques.

I completed my Bachelor’s degree from the University of the Punjab, Lahore, followed by a Master’s degree in Mathematics from Riphah International University, Islamabad. Later, I earned my M.Phil. in Mathematics from Capital University of Science and Technology (CUST), Islamabad. My M.Phil. research focused on the analysis of hybrid nanofluid flow containing motile microorganisms under the Cattaneo–Christov double diffusion model, where I applied numerical methods to study complex fluid flow phenomena.

Currently, my doctoral research involves the development and application of advanced numerical techniques to analyze nonlinear fluid flow systems and heat transfer mechanisms. I regularly use scientific computing tools such as MATLAB, Mathematica, and LaTeX for modeling, simulation, and research documentation.

Through my academic work, I strive to contribute to innovative research in fluid dynamics, applied mathematics, and energy-efficient technologies, while also engaging in scholarly collaboration and academic exchange with researchers around the world.
